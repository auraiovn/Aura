The collection guide

A considered guide to virtual try-on fashion

Our latest collection brings editorial fashion and practical decision-making into the same space. Browse first, use the fitting tool when it helps, and purchase through a familiar product flow.

AURA’s new arrivals are built around proportion: a long trench that holds its line, a fluid dress that changes with movement, a structured blazer that makes a quiet statement, and knitwear designed for repeated wear. Rather than treating digital tools as a spectacle, our virtual try-on fashion experience helps you answer three simple questions. Does the silhouette suit the way I like to dress? Does this colour work with what I already own? Which size should I inspect more closely before adding it to my bag?

How to choose a silhouette online

Begin with the product’s intended fit. A relaxed garment should not be judged by the same visual cues as a close-cut piece. On each product page, AURA explains the model’s size, garment measurements, fabric behaviour and styling intention. Open Live Try-On to compare overall length, shoulder position and colour near your face. The view is directional rather than a tailoring guarantee, so the measurement table remains the most reliable source for your final size choice. This combination keeps virtual try-on fashion useful without pretending that a camera can replace every physical detail.

For outerwear, pay attention to layering room. Measure a coat you already enjoy from shoulder to shoulder and compare it with the garment measurements. For dresses, compare both bust and hip measurements, then read whether the fabric has stretch. For knitwear, look at the intended ease and fibre composition. These steps are fast, repeatable and available without opening your camera.

Privacy and accessibility by design

Camera access is optional. If you launch Live Try-On, the browser asks for permission at that moment and shows a visible live indicator. In this prototype, the camera feed stays on the device and is not stored. You can close the experience, revoke access and continue with images, video, measurements and our size guide. Keyboard navigation, clear focus states, readable contrast and large controls support people who interact in different ways.

A technology feature should remove uncertainty, not create a new barrier. That is why every blue button means technology or experimentation, while every black button means a shopping transaction. The same colour logic follows you from collection to product, cart and checkout. You can recognise an action before reading every word.

Building a wardrobe with fewer wrong turns

Start with the pieces that connect to at least three things you already own. A sand blazer may complete a dress, soften denim and add shape to knitwear. A neutral trench can bridge work and weekend styling. Use the “Complete the Look” section for ideas, then edit them against your real wardrobe rather than buying an entire styled set by default. Virtual try-on fashion is most valuable when it supports a more deliberate choice.

Our collection pages place products before editorial text, because shopping should remain the primary task. This deeper guide sits below the browsing experience for customers who want more context and for search engines that need meaningful, original information. We avoid repeating empty keywords or hiding important facts. Each page should answer a real product question, use clear headings and connect directly to size, delivery, returns or support.

From screen to fitting room

AURA is an omnichannel system. Online shoppers use the browser experience, while partner retailers can bring the same product language into stores through a 55-inch Smart Mirror. A shopper can explore an item in-store, scan a QR code and continue on a phone without rebuilding the cart. The goal is continuity: the product, size and colour remain understandable across channels.

Reading product evidence well

Online confidence depends on the quality of the evidence, not the number of decorative images. Start with the front, side and back views, then inspect close details for weave, lining, seams and fastening. A movement film should reveal how the material folds and returns rather than repeat the campaign image. Product descriptions should state what a piece is made from, how it is intended to fit and what care it requires. Reviews add another layer when they identify the purchased size and remain tied to a verified order.

Stock information also changes the decision. “In stock” is useful; “only four remaining in size M” is clearer. Delivery estimates should reflect the selected destination and appear before the cart. Return conditions belong beside the decision rather than behind a legal link. AURA keeps these elements close to the colour, size and action controls so customers can resolve uncertainty without leaving the product page.

Using Live Try-On responsibly

Place the device on a stable surface, use an uncluttered background and stand far enough away for the relevant part of the body to remain visible. Avoid interpreting small visual differences as exact measurements. Camera angle, loose clothing and lighting can affect the view. Use the feature to compare direction—longer or shorter, warmer or cooler, structured or fluid—then confirm the final size with garment measurements and fit notes.

If camera access is unavailable, nothing essential disappears. The product gallery, film transcript, measurement table, colour options, stock, delivery and purchase controls remain available. This progressive approach also protects page speed: core shopping content loads first, while camera code begins only after a deliberate action. Images use responsive delivery and lazy loading below the first viewport to reduce unnecessary transfer.

After the purchase

The order confirmation should repeat the item, size, colour, delivery destination and expected timeline in plain language. Care guidance follows the product so the customer can find it again. If the piece is not right, the return path explains the condition, deadline, label and refund timing before asking for a reason. That reason can improve product information, but it should never be used to obstruct a lawful return.

Browse the collection by category, filter for Live-enabled pieces, open any product and decide which evidence you need. You might rely on photography and measurements for one piece, then use virtual try-on fashion for another. Both routes lead to the same accessible cart and transparent checkout. If you still need help, choose “Shopper” on the contact page and your message will be routed to the customer-care team.

Enterprise FAQ

Before it enters your store.

Does the mirror replace store associates?

No. It extends the associate's product and inventory context while the associate continues to provide human styling and service.

What systems need to connect?

At minimum, a clean product catalogue and inventory feed. Cart, CRM and analytics connections depend on trial scope and consent design.

How is shopper privacy handled?

The interface discloses camera use, resets sessions, avoids facial identification and supports a browsing path without AR. Final deployment follows the retailer's legal and security review.

What does the 14-day trial include?

Site survey, one trial unit, configuration, staff onboarding, support and an agreed evaluation report. Commercial terms are confirmed before installation.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an app for Live Try-On?

No. It opens in a supported mobile or desktop browser. Camera access is requested only when you start the experience.

Can I shop without using my camera?

Yes. Product imagery, video, garment measurements, the size guide and checkout all work without Live Try-On.

How accurate is the view?

It is a directional styling and proportion tool, not a made-to-measure guarantee. Always compare garment measurements before choosing a size.

How can a retailer test the Smart Mirror?

Open Enterprise from the main navigation, review the process and submit the 14-day trial form.

Shipping information

Tracked delivery

Estimated delivery dates appear on each product page and again at checkout. Complimentary standard delivery applies to orders above $150. Duties and taxes are shown before payment where supported.

Order updates

After confirmation, customers receive an order reference and tracking link. Contact support if a parcel has not moved for three working days.

Return policy

30 days to decide

Return unworn items with original tags within 30 days of delivery. Start the return online, choose a reason and use the provided label where available. Refunds return to the original payment method after inspection.

Exceptions

Personalised pieces, hygiene-sensitive items and visibly worn garments cannot be returned unless faulty. Statutory rights remain unaffected.

Size, use & care

Measure first

Compare bust, waist, hip and garment length with a similar piece you own. Live Try-On adds visual context but does not replace the measurement table.

Make it last

Follow the care label, air garments between wears and use the repair details supplied on each product page.

Privacy

Camera choice

Live Try-On requires explicit browser permission. This academic prototype does not upload, identify or store camera imagery. A future commercial version would require a published retention policy, processor list and regional legal review.

Contact and checkout data

Forms in this prototype validate locally and display a success state only. No submission is sent to a database.

Terms

This website is a concept prototype for academic evaluation. Product availability, prices, reviews, team profiles, hardware specifications and trial terms are illustrative until verified for a commercial launch.

The interface must not be used to infer body measurements, identity, health or protected characteristics.
